Visuals & aesthetics Prototypes

The first area I would like to explore is the visual and aesthetic experience of my instrument. Currently, my interaction is quite simple, with a corresponding animation appearing on the screen when a letter on the keyboard is pressed. I initially wanted to keep the experience very minimalistic by keeping the animations very simple through regular shapes and colours. This would make the interaction more easy to understand and for toddlers to create a connection between their input causing the output. However, I want to experiment with visuals that could be more engaging and meaningful for toddlers. I would like to explore whether I could introduce an educational element, such as helping toddlers recognise letters, colours or shapes through the visual design.

Another aspect I'm concerned about is the plain background along with the lack of written instructions and how it will communicate the purpose of the site. This makes it unclear to a toddler what they are supposed to do when they first interact with the site. A more inviting or interactive visual environment could provide clues on how to start and how to operate the instrument. I'm also considering the short attention spans of toddlers, as they can lose interest with repetitive interactions overtime. Therefor, I would like to explore visuals that have more dynamic and could introduce additional features or interactions, encouraging more consistent enagagement. Implementing these approaches would allow me to consider different backgrounds and visuals that can make the instrument more playful, interactive and potentially educational too.

Prototype 1: Visual Layout

For my first variation, I took inspiration from Patatap, where each letter corresponds to a specific animation and rainbow. The sounds include different musical instruments and sound effects commonly used in modern music. For this prototype, I replaced the animations with images that appear when each letter is pressed and also made the background colours change.

Prototype 3: Visual Layout

For this prototype, I wanted to explore a different scenery and visual style while also changing the audio feedback. When a user presses a key, a corresponding jellyfish image and sound appears on the screen. The sound effect repeats twice and the jellyfish stays visible for longer than in other prototypes. This creates a more lingering, echo-like effect for both visual and audio aspects, to reflect the underwater and ocean aesthetic of the prototype.

Prototype 4: Use of Technique

This prototype is a variation of Prototype 1, where I used the initial code and introduced the technique of randomness by making the image size & pitch randomly assigned. I created 3 image sizes (small, medium & large) which would be paired with a different pitch (small images produce a higher-pitched sound, medium - original pitch, & large - lower-pitch). This creates a stronger connection between the visual & audio feedback.
