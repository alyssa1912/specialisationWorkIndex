Project Synopsis

This project is an interactive musical instrument designed for toddlers, allowing them to create sounds by pressing letter keys on a computer keyboard. Each key produces a different sound and displays a different star animation, allowing children to build their own colourful night sky as they explore music through play. The instrument combines sound, movement, and simple visual feedback to create an engaging and intuitive experience without relying on written instructions or musical knowledge. The project encourages young children to experiment with sounds, discover cause and effect, and enjoy creative musical exploration.

Design Scenario and Response


Chosen Scenario: Musical Toy for Toddlers

Prompt - Design a way for young children with minimal language skills to explore music.

The design scenario explores how many browser-based instruments are powerful tools for creating music and are often designed for adults or experienced users who mostly rely on written instructions or their understanding of musical concepts to create compositions. While this offers a high level of creative freedom, they are not accessible, engaging or comprehensible for younger users such as toddlers, as many of these interfaces require users to interpret text-based instructions, navigate menus and understand musical concepts before they can interact with the instrument. For toddlers whose language and motor skills are still developing, this can be a barrier that discourages them from using these tools.

My project responds to the scenario by creating a simple, interactive musical instrument that allows toddlers to explore sound through experimentation. By pressing different keys on a keyboard, children can trigger unique sounds paired with exciting visual animations. This creates a direct connection between their actions and the audio-visual feedback they receive. The instrument uses playful animations, visually aesthetic themes and simple interactions to make the experience engaging and approachable for young children. As toddlers have limited language skills, the design reduces reliance on text and communicates through visual cues, movement and sound. The experience encourages curiosity and independent exploration without requiring musical knowledge or complicated controls.

Core Interactive Features & Design System Documentation


1. Keyboard Interaction and Sound

I chose the keyboard as the main form of interaction for the instrument to keep the interactions simple & straightforward. Users are not required to navigate complex menus or controls, supporting the project's purpose of making music approachable for young audiences. Each letter key has a corresponding sound and I included a variety of unique sound effects to make the experience more playful and encourage creative exploration. Keys A-N produce notes that gradually change in pitch, then keys Q-T introduce a different sound effect, available in four different pitches. This variety encourages experimentation and keeps the musical experience playful and engaging.

Visual Design and Animations

I chose a colourful, visual style to create an inviting environment that encourages toddlers to explore music. The night-sky theme, stars and animated elements give the instrument a sense of playfulness, making the experience feel more like an interactive toy than a traditional musical instrument. I used fun visual animations to provide feedback to capture children's attention, encourage curiosity and make music exploration enjoyable.

Introductory Modal

During development, I realised that my original introductory modal relied mainly on text, which could make it difficult for toddlers with limited reading skills to understand. To address this, I introduced visual imagery to make it much easier to understand. I did this to meet my target audience's needs by reducing reliance on written language, and instead using visual cues to communicate a clear starting point to users.

Extended technique: Math.random()

I implemented this technique because it introduces variation into the instrument's visual feedback, making the experience less predictable and encouraging continued exploration. During the exploration stage, I used this technique to assign a random position to the stars when a key was pressed, allowed the star to appear in different positions rather than following a fixed pattern. In the final instrument, I chose to apply Math.random() to image selection. When a key is pressed, one of 8 available star images can be randomly selected and displayed. This meant the same key could produce different visual results when interacted with. I felt that the implementation of this technique encourages curiosity and playful experimentation while still keeping the interaction simple.
